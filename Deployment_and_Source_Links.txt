SUGGESTED WALKTHROUGH FOR ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.  Open the live application. It lands on the sign-in page; follow "Browse the
    archive" to read the four published papers without an account. Each carries a
    downloadable PDF, a DOI-shaped identifier, BibTeX/RIS citation export, and a
    provenance panel that verifies its editorial history against the audit chain.

2.  Search the archive. Search is PostgreSQL full-text over the extracted body
    text of each PDF, so a distinctive term from inside a paper returns it with a
    highlighted snippet.

3.  Sign in as the Author. Submit a manuscript with a PDF. The response reports
    what the anonymiser stripped from the file and flags any author name it can
    still find in the body text.

4.  Sign in as the Editor. The screening queue shows the new submission. Open the
    reviewer-candidate list: candidates are ranked by expertise match against the
    manuscript's keywords, and excluded candidates appear with their reason
    (shared affiliation, at capacity) rather than being hidden. Assign two
    reviewers and note the deadlines and overdue flags.

5.  Sign in as a Reviewer. The manuscript appears in blinded form: no author
    name, affiliation or identifier. This is enforced by the type the API
    returns, which has no author fields at all, not by filtering at the edge.
    Submit a structured review: four 1-5 criterion scores, comments to the
    author, and confidential comments to the editor.

6.  Back as the Editor, read both reviews (only this role can see the
    confidential channel) and record a decision. Download the decision
    certificate, a PDF carrying the decision and the audit chain's head hash.
    View the analytics dashboard for pipeline counts, acceptance rate and
    turnaround times.

7.  As the Author, an accepted manuscript raises an article processing charge.
    The deployment uses a mock payment gateway by default, so "Pay" settles the
    invoice immediately and no card is involved. No real money moves through this
    system.

8.  Sign in as the Editor-in-Chief to schedule the paper into an issue and
    publish it, or to waive the charge.

9.  Sign in as the Administrator to see the accounts console: grant and revoke
    roles, set reviewer capacity, deactivate accounts. Two actions are refused by
    design, to stop the last administrator locking everyone out: the
    administrator role cannot be changed here, and you cannot deactivate
    yourself.

NOTES FOR THE EXAMINER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Tests: 402 backend unit, 84 integration against a real PostgreSQL container,
  and 79 frontend, all passing. Domain and application coverage is 90.03%
  against an 85% gate. Verified by GitHub Actions run 31791711326.

- The deployed architecture is smaller than the design document specifies (ECS
  Fargate behind an Application Load Balancer with CloudFront). That decision,
  its reasoning and its repayment cost are recorded as TD-14 in the technical
  debt register.

- The technical debt register carries fifteen entries across three categories:
  critical, scheduled, and acceptable. Every inadvertent entry was found by
  review of code that had already passed linting, strict type checking, an
  architecture contract and a full test suite; the rest are deliberate
  trade-offs, priced when they were taken.

- Payments are mocked by default. A real Paystack integration exists and is
  exercised only when a secret key is configured; no key is deployed, and no
  payment has ever been taken.

- The application must remain reachable for grading. The AWS infrastructure is
  billed continuously at roughly USD 29 per month while running.
